# The 4096-light plan — derived from budget, not taste

> ⚠️ **SUPERSEDED IN ITS PRIORITIES by [I8](issues.md#i8) (2026-07-27).** A per-pass GPU profile showed the
> cost is the **fine lightmap bake (9.7 ms)**, not the shadow gather (**1.6 ms**) — and inside the bake, the
> per-fine-texel **edge refine is 73 % of the whole GPU frame** and is a toggle. So **P1 below (the per-texel
> walk budget) targets a pass worth 14 % of the frame**, and P5 (the 2.79× oversample), which this document
> ranks last, is actually the biggest structural lever because it multiplies the dominant pass. The budget
> arithmetic in §2 stands; the assignment of it to passes does not. Revised order: **edge refine → lightmap
> resolution → `buildCasters` CPU → walk budget.** Left otherwise unedited so the mis-attribution is legible.

_Target (user, 2026-07-26): **4096 lights in motion, reach 16 tiles, 60 fps, integrated GPU.**
Every number below traces to that constraint. Written to end the redesign cycle: the architecture is whatever
the arithmetic permits, and where the target is not physically reachable this says so explicitly rather than
promising it._

## 1. What is already solved (do not rebuild it)

**Light count is ALREADY decoupled from GPU cost.** `light_presence` keeps **the nearest ≤16 lights per tile**
and `accumulateLights` loops only those. 4096 lights cost a shader exactly what 16 cost. The measured
scaling — 11/35/67 moving lights at 13.6/41.2/75.2 ms — is *not* the presence cap failing; it is the
**walk** getting longer as lights spread out. So "4096 lights" is not the hard part.

**The lighting itself is free.** 35 moving lights measured **43.23 ms casting / 8.33 ms not casting**
([I6](issues.md#i6)) — the entire 4096 × 2048 lightmap bake, falloff, N·L and accumulation sit under the vsync
floor. **≥81 % of the frame is the shadow walk.** Optimising anything else is optimising the free half.

**The dirty system, the projection math, the max-accumulate invariant, the additive accumulator, and the
corridor↔brute identity harness all stay.** Nothing below replaces the renderer.

## 2. The budget

| quantity | measured |
|---|---|
| shadow gather runs at | **512 × 256 = 131 072 texels** (`TEXTILE_UNIT`, 16/tile over a 32 × 16-tile map) |
| lightmap runs at | 4096 × 2048 = 8.39 M texels |
| canvas | 2560 × 1172 = 3.00 M px |
| today's worst case per shadow texel | ≤16 lights × up to **64**-tile walk × 3 fetches = **up to 3 072 fetches** |
| today's cost, 32 moving reach-16 lights | **149 ms** |

An integrated GPU gives roughly 50–60 GB/s. Allocating **8 ms** of a 16.67 ms frame to lighting and assuming
realistic efficiency leaves on the order of **25–50 M texture fetches per frame**. Today's worst case is
`131 072 × 3 072 ≈ 400 M`. **We are ~8–16× over budget, and that is the whole problem.**

Dividing the budget back out:

> **~25 M fetches ÷ 131 072 texels ÷ 3 fetches per step = ~64 walk-steps per texel, TOTAL, across all of its
> lights.**

That single number is the design constraint. Everything in §3 follows from it.

## 3. The plan

### P1 — A per-texel WALK BUDGET (the whole win, and the smallest change)
Today each of ≤16 present lights independently walks up to 64 tiles. Instead, give the **texel** a fixed
budget of ~64 steps and spend it nearest-light-first: the closest light walks its full corridor, and each
subsequent light gets what remains.

- Cost becomes **constant per texel regardless of light count** — the property the target needs.
- It degrades in the right direction. **3 sparse torches:** each affords a full 16-tile walk, so today's
  long dramatic shadows are unchanged. **4096 dense lights:** the nearest 16 are all within a tile or two, so
  their corridors are naturally short and the budget is not even binding.
- Expected: `400 M → 25 M` fetches, **~8–16×**, which is precisely the gap.
- Cheap: a step counter threaded through `walkShadow`'s caller, no new data structures, no new state.

**This is the phase that hits the target.** It is deliberately first because it is also the smallest.

### P2 — A coarse tier for the culled lights (correctness, not speed)
With 4096 lights reaching every tile, presence keeps 16 and **silently drops 4 080**. Each dropped light is
dim, but 4 080 of them are not: the world would be visibly darker than it should be, and lights would pop as
the top-16 set changed. Bake **all** lights, unshadowed, into a **1-texel-per-tile** map (512 texels;
4096 × 512 = 2 M evaluations, no fetches — negligible) and add it under the exact tier.

Without this, "4096 lights" is a lie: only 16 of them per tile would ever be visible.

### P3 — Shadow only the dominant lights
Once P2 supplies the ambient sum, only the top **S ≈ 4** lights per tile need to cast. A light contributing
1/4096 of a texel's illumination cannot produce a visible shadow. Halves or quarters P1's budget again and is
physically justified rather than a quality cut.

### P4 — Incremental presence maintenance
`buildPresence`/`buildCasters` scan every in-window tile every frame. That is fine at 3 lights and quadratic
misery at 4096 movers — and it is **CPU** time, so it caps the frame independently of everything above. The
design already exists, unbuilt, in
[`2026-07-23-incremental-presence`](../2026-07-23-incremental-presence/README.md): a bidirectional light↔tile
index, upsert-during-dirty, full rebuild only on pan. **Measure the CPU share before building it** — it may
not bind until light counts are in the hundreds.

### P5 — Size the lightmap to the canvas
The 2.79× oversample ([I4](issues.md#i4)). Last, because it applies to the half that is already free — it buys
headroom for P2/P3 rather than frame time on its own. I previously called this the next best lever, which was
wrong by ~5:1.

## 4. What this does NOT deliver, stated plainly

**4096 lights each casting its own full-length shadows is not reachable, on any hardware, by any algorithm.**
Reach 16 covers ~804 tiles against a 512-tile map, so every one of the 4096 lights reaches every tile: that is
2 M light-tile pairs of *genuinely distinct* shadow geometry per frame. The information content itself exceeds
the budget — no data structure compresses it, because none of it is redundant.

What the plan delivers: **4096 lights that all illuminate, with shadows from the dominant few per texel, at a
cost that is constant in light count.** Visually that is what 4096 lights look like anyway, because the
non-dominant ones cannot cast a perceptible shadow. If the real requirement is 4096 *individually
shadow-casting* lights, the honest answer is that the target needs to change, not the architecture.

## 5. Explicitly rejected

- **Restore the 2026-07-21 per-light caster LUT.** No spatial pruning: a reach-16 light's list holds ~500
  casters at this world's density, all tested at every texel behind only a box cull. Worse than the walk.
- **Per-light SHADOW buckets** ([F4](forks.md#f4) option c). Attractive — it keeps pruning *and* restores the
  light association — but maintenance is `Σ lights × casters-in-reach × shadow tiles` ≈ **9.8 M writes/frame**
  at 4096 lights, on the CPU, in JS. It trades a GPU problem for a worse CPU one. Viable only if the shadow
  set is first capped by P3, at which point P1 has already won.
- **Per-light 1D radial or 2D shadow maps.** ~65 k texels per light × 4096 = 268 M texels of storage. Dead on
  arrival for memory alone, before the build cost.
- **Screen-space per-pixel light evaluation.** ~3 M px × N lights ≫ the baked map's 131 k shadow texels.

## 6. Acceptance

The target is a measurement, run with the existing harness (deterministic light placement, asserted
`carriedLights.size`, output read via `debugReadShadow`):

- **4096 moving lights, reach 16, zoom 1 and 0.5, ≥60 fps** on the integrated GPU.
- Corridor↔brute identity holds where the walk is unbudgeted (0 mismatches, non-zero population both sides).
- A budgeted walk is **not** expected to be bit-identical to an unbudgeted one — that is the approximation. It
  must instead be shown **stable**: no flicker as the budget reallocates between frames, verified by hashing
  the shadow map across frames with lights static.
